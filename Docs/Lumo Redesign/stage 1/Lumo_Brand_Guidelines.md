# LUMO — Brand Guidelines

> **Status:** Locked  
> **Purpose:** Source of truth for Lumo's brand identity, visual personality, and brand expression.

---

# 1. Brand Overview

## Brand Name

**Lumo**

Always write the product name as:

> **Lumo**

Do not use:

- LUMO (except where the logo itself uses uppercase lettering)
- Lumo AI
- Lumo Tutor
- Lumo Learning AI
- The Lumo App

The product brand is simply **Lumo**.

---

## Brand Essence

Lumo is an AI-native learning environment designed to make difficult subjects easier to understand through:

- personalized teaching
- conversational interaction
- voice
- visual explanations
- interactive lessons
- adaptive questioning
- constructive feedback
- multilingual learning

Lumo should feel less like an educational website and more like a **place where learning happens**.

### Core idea

> **Lumo helps students understand, not merely consume information.**

---

# 2. Brand Positioning

Lumo is NOT:

- a chatbot with an educational skin
- a generic ed-tech dashboard
- a video library
- a gamified children's application
- a talking avatar placed over text
- a collection of AI-generated worksheets

Lumo IS:

> **A calm, intelligent, visual learning environment with a personal AI tutor.**

The interface should make the product's intelligence visible.

The user should be able to intuitively understand:

> "This system is teaching me."

rather than:

> "This system is generating content for me."

---

# 3. Brand Personality

Lumo's personality is defined by five qualities.

## 3.1 Intelligent

Lumo should feel capable, thoughtful, and knowledgeable.

The product should communicate intelligence through:

- clear explanations
- meaningful visualizations
- contextual responses
- adaptive teaching
- thoughtful interactions

Avoid trying to communicate intelligence through excessive futuristic effects.

---

## 3.2 Calm

Learning can already be mentally demanding.

Lumo should reduce unnecessary visual and cognitive pressure.

Use:

- generous negative space
- clear hierarchy
- restrained motion
- readable typography
- predictable interaction patterns
- uncluttered layouts

The interface should never feel frantic.

---

## 3.3 Curious

Lumo should encourage exploration.

Use:

- interesting visual demonstrations
- interactive diagrams
- thoughtful questions
- progressive discovery
- contextual examples
- subtle moments of delight

The goal is to make the student want to discover:

> "What happens next?"

---

## 3.4 Human

Lumo is an AI tutor, not a machine interface.

The product should feel:

- encouraging
- patient
- conversational
- responsive
- constructive

When a student struggles, the experience should communicate:

> "Let's figure this out."

Never:

> "You failed."

---

## 3.5 Cinematic

Visual teaching is one of Lumo's major differentiators.

Lessons can contain:

- animated diagrams
- scientific visualizations
- simulations
- environments
- timelines
- maps
- mathematical visualizations
- code execution
- 2D and 3D demonstrations

However, cinematic design must always serve understanding.

> **Animation should explain, not distract.**

---

# 4. Core Brand Philosophy

## Calm × Curious × Cinematic

These three words define Lumo's visual identity.

### Calm

The interface gives the student room to think.

### Curious

The interface makes concepts interesting to explore.

### Cinematic

The teaching experience can become visually rich when the concept benefits from it.

These qualities must coexist.

Do not sacrifice clarity for visual spectacle.

---

# 5. The Lumo Principle

Every major design decision should pass this test:

> **Does this help the student understand, navigate, or feel supported?**

If the answer is no, question whether the element belongs.

This is especially important for:

- decorative illustrations
- animations
- gradients
- badges
- statistics
- gamification
- floating elements
- background effects

Lumo should prefer **intentional simplicity over decorative complexity**.

---

# 6. Logo

## Primary Logo

The Lumo logo consists of:

- the Lumo symbol
- the Lumo wordmark

The logo is intentionally simple and geometric.

It should remain visually clean and recognizable at small sizes.

---

## Light Theme Logo

Use the **dark/black Lumo wordmark and symbol**.

The logo should sit comfortably against the light/friendly Lumo background.

---

## Dark Theme Logo

Use the **white Lumo wordmark and symbol**.

Do not force the dark logo onto dark backgrounds.

---

## Logo Treatment

The logo should generally appear:

- flat
- clean
- high contrast
- unmodified
- free from unnecessary effects

Do not add:

- drop shadows
- glow
- gradients
- excessive outlines
- glass effects
- decorative animation

unless a very specific branded animation is intentionally designed.

---

## Logo Clear Space

Maintain sufficient breathing room around the logo.

Never allow:

- navigation elements
- text
- cards
- decorative illustrations
- buttons

to visually collide with the logo.

The logo should have enough negative space to remain immediately recognizable.

---

## Logo Don'ts

Do not:

- stretch the logo
- compress the logo
- rotate it
- distort proportions
- recolor it arbitrarily
- add random gradients
- place it over low-contrast imagery
- add unnecessary effects
- redesign the symbol for individual pages

The logo is a fixed brand asset.

---

# 7. Light Theme Identity

The light theme is a major part of Lumo's personality.

It should feel:

> **Fruity, fresh, warm, optimistic, premium, and calm.**

"Fruity" means expressive color accents and playful visual energy — not a childish interface.

---

## Light Theme Foundation

The foundation should be:

- warm white
- ivory
- soft neutral surfaces

Avoid making the entire interface pure white.

The slight warmth helps create a softer, more editorial learning environment.

---

## Light Theme Color Personality

The primary visual energy should come from warm colors such as:

- orange
- coral
- peach
- soft yellow

Supported by:

- mint
- fresh green
- sky blue
- occasional pink/lavender

These colors should appear as **accents**, not as competing backgrounds everywhere.

---

## Light Theme Rules

The majority of the interface should remain neutral.

Use color to:

- establish hierarchy
- highlight important information
- indicate interaction
- reinforce learning states
- create visual warmth
- add moments of delight

Do not turn every component into a colorful object.

---

# 8. Dark Theme Identity

The dark theme should feel:

> **Premium, cinematic, immersive, and intelligent.**

It should not feel:

- cyberpunk
- hacker-themed
- overly technical
- neon-heavy
- aggressively futuristic

---

## Dark Theme Foundation

Use deep neutral backgrounds rather than pure black wherever possible.

The interface should have enough tonal variation to establish hierarchy between:

- background
- surface
- elevated surface
- interactive surface
- borders
- content

---

## Dark Theme Color Personality

The same Lumo brand colors remain available in dark mode.

However, colors should be adjusted for:

- contrast
- readability
- visual balance
- reduced eye strain

Warm Lumo colors can become especially effective in dark mode as controlled highlights.

---

## Dark Theme Logo

The logo should switch to the **white version**.

The brand remains the same.

Only the visual treatment adapts to the environment.

---

# 9. Fruity Does Not Mean Chaotic

This distinction is critical.

Lumo can use:

- colorful illustrations
- fruit-inspired accent colors
- colorful diagrams
- playful highlights
- soft gradients
- expressive visual elements

But the underlying structure must remain calm.

### Correct

```text
Neutral foundation
        +
One dominant accent
        +
Small supporting colors
        +
Generous whitespace
```

````

### Incorrect

```text
Orange card
Green card
Blue card
Pink card
Yellow button
Purple background
Gradient text
Floating icons
Particles everywhere
```

Color should create **moments**, not noise.

---

# 10. Negative Space

Negative space is a deliberate part of the Lumo brand.

Do not automatically fill empty areas.

Empty space can communicate:

- confidence
- premium quality
- focus
- calm
- hierarchy

A page should never be made "better" simply by adding more elements.

### Rule

> **If an element does not improve the experience, leave the space empty.**

---

# 11. Visual Hierarchy

Every screen should have a clear visual priority.

The user should immediately understand:

1. Where am I?
2. What am I looking at?
3. What should I do next?

Avoid layouts where:

- multiple cards compete equally
- multiple buttons demand attention
- decorative elements overpower content
- every section uses an accent color
- everything is animated

---

# 12. AI Tutor Brand Expression

The AI tutor is part of the Lumo experience but is not the entire product.

The tutor should feel:

- approachable
- expressive
- intelligent
- patient
- present

The tutor may eventually use:

- realistic or stylized 3D characters
- facial expressions
- lip synchronization
- gestures
- natural voice
- contextual reactions

However:

> **The AI tutor guides the lesson. The lesson content remains the hero.**

The tutor should not permanently occupy the majority of the screen when the visual explanation is more important.

---

# 13. Visual Learning Identity

Lumo's most important visual content is not decorative artwork.

It is **teaching visualization**.

Examples:

### Physics

- force vectors
- moving objects
- simulations
- real-world environments
- animated equations

### Biology

- cells
- organs
- biological processes
- 3D structures

### Chemistry

- molecules
- reactions
- atomic structures

### Mathematics

- graphs
- geometry
- equations
- spatial relationships

### History

- timelines
- maps
- environments
- artifacts

### Geography

- maps
- terrain
- climate
- spatial visualization

### Programming

- code
- execution flow
- architecture
- data structures
- system diagrams

The visual language should evolve according to the subject while remaining recognizably Lumo.

---

# 14. Brand Do's

## DO

- Prioritize clarity.
- Use generous negative space.
- Use warm, expressive colors in the light theme.
- Keep the dark theme premium and restrained.
- Use color intentionally.
- Make learning content visually interesting.
- Use animation when it communicates something.
- Make the AI feel supportive.
- Make the product feel intelligent without looking stereotypically "AI."
- Maintain consistent visual language across the application.
- Let the lesson content become the visual hero.
- Design for both younger and older learners through adaptive expression rather than completely different products.
- Keep interactions predictable.
- Make complex concepts feel approachable.

---

# 15. Brand Don'ts

## DON'T

- Use indigo/purple AI gradients as the default visual identity.
- Make the product look like generic AI SaaS.
- Fill every empty space.
- Use excessive glassmorphism.
- Use excessive neon.
- Turn every section into a card.
- Make everything colorful.
- Overuse gradients.
- Overuse shadows.
- Over-round every component.
- Use childish cartoon aesthetics as the default.
- Use generic stock illustrations.
- Use random AI-generated decorative artwork.
- Add animation simply because animation is possible.
- Use confetti, XP, streaks, badges, or gamification everywhere.
- Make the AI avatar the sole visual focus.
- Duplicate narration with unnecessary large blocks of identical text.
- Use color as the only indicator of meaning.
- Allow AI-generated pages to invent their own visual language.

---

# 16. Lumo vs Generic AI Design

Avoid the following visual stereotypes:

```text
❌ Purple/indigo gradient
❌ Glowing blobs
❌ Glassmorphism everywhere
❌ Neon borders
❌ AI sparkle icons everywhere
❌ Floating 3D brain
❌ Robot imagery
❌ Excessive futuristic grids
❌ Dark dashboard by default
```

Lumo should instead communicate intelligence through the **quality of the experience**.

```text
✓ Clear hierarchy
✓ Beautiful visual explanations
✓ Intelligent interactions
✓ Purposeful animation
✓ Adaptive teaching
✓ Natural voice
✓ Thoughtful feedback
✓ Subject-aware visuals
```

---

# 17. Lumo's Emotional Test

Before approving a major screen, ask:

### Does it feel calm?

Would a student feel comfortable spending 30–60 minutes here?

### Does it feel curious?

Does the interface encourage exploration?

### Does it feel intelligent?

Does the product demonstrate intelligence through behavior rather than decoration?

### Does it feel human?

Does the student feel supported rather than evaluated constantly?

### Does it feel premium?

Does the design look intentional rather than assembled from generic components?

### Does it feel like Lumo?

Could this screen belong to another AI product without changing anything?

If yes, the design needs more Lumo identity.

---

# 18. Design North Star

All future Lumo design decisions should move toward:

> **A calm, beautiful, AI-native learning environment that makes difficult subjects feel visually understandable.**

The product should make students think:

> **"Oh... studying here actually feels easy."**

And judges should immediately understand:

> **"This isn't just a chatbot. This is an AI teacher and learning environment."**

---

# 19. Final Brand Rule

When forced to choose between:

**More visual spectacle**

and

**better learning clarity**

choose:

> **Better learning clarity.**

When forced to choose between:

**filling empty space**

and

**preserving negative space**

choose:

> **Preserve the negative space.**

When forced to choose between:

**looking like generic AI**

and

**building a distinct Lumo identity**

choose:

> **Distinct Lumo identity.**

When forced to choose between:

**a beautiful interface**

and

**a beautiful learning experience**

choose:

> **The beautiful learning experience.**
````
